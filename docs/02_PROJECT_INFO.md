# Project information

## Overview

ProofBridge is a proposed academia–industry collaboration portal for skill mapping, internships and placements. It helps students demonstrate ability, employers review relevant work and colleges respond to gaps in practical preparation. It is designed first as a focused hackathon prototype and subsequently as a small institutional pilot.

The official problem statement is reproduced from the team: “Portal for Academia-industry collaboration for skill mapping, internships and placement.” The team reports selection for the IIC 3.0 MUJ final under Open Innovation with an EdTech problem statement. This document does not independently verify event rules or deadlines.

## Problem statement for the presentation

Students, colleges and employers often work with disconnected information: course results, resumes, projects, internship listings and placement spreadsheets. A skill listed on a resume does not show how it was demonstrated. A job requirement does not always tell a student how to improve. A college may see final placement counts without seeing the practical skill gaps behind them. ProofBridge proposes a shared workflow that links requirements to reviewed evidence and subsequent opportunities.

## Proposed solution

An employer publishes a role with explicit skills and proficiency requirements. A student sees their reviewed evidence against those requirements and can choose a relevant challenge. A human reviewer assesses the work using a visible rubric. The student's evidence passport and role-specific coverage then update. Recruiters can inspect evidence during shortlisting, while a college sees aggregate gaps and pipeline progress for its participating students.

## Users and benefits

Students receive a clearer path from learning to applications, feedback on actual work and control over evidence sharing. Employers receive structured evidence and a repeatable review process. Faculty receive examples of practical strengths and gaps. Placement teams receive organized applications and outcome records. Platform administrators handle organization approval, moderation and exceptional access.

## Example journey

Meera, a fictional MCA student, is interested in an analyst internship. Her spreadsheet and communication evidence meet most requirements, but she has no reviewed SQL evidence. She completes a two-hour synthetic-data challenge. The reviewer records SQL level 3 with explanations. Meera sees her updated coverage and applies. The employer reviews the artifact and decides whether to invite her. The institution sees an aggregate reduction in the selected cohort's SQL evidence gap.

## Why it is worth building

The project makes collaboration visible in the actual product: industry supplies requirements, students supply work, reviewers supply feedback and colleges supply learning support. It can be demonstrated without claiming access to proprietary hiring data or a large external job network. Its value is a testable workflow improvement, not an assertion that an AI model can determine a person's career.

## What the prototype includes

Four role-oriented workspaces; approved organizations; a small skill catalog; opportunities and associated challenges; evidence submission; human rubric review; an evidence passport; deterministic gap mapping; application status tracking; basic cohort analytics; and an audit trail. In-app notifications and one simple internship outcome record are included when the core flow is stable.

## What the prototype does not claim

It is not a job guarantee, a replacement for interviews, an accredited credential issuer or an automatic university curriculum designer. Synthetic employer records are not partnerships. A job simulation is not employment. Industry-supplied tasks require permission and appropriate scope. External internship portals are research context, not connected feeds in the prototype.

## Outcomes and measurement

Measure how many students complete reviewed evidence, how long reviews take, whether recruiters can explain a shortlist decision, how many applications reach interviews and whether college staff use the gap view. Compare with a small baseline workflow before claiming improvement. Record candidate experience and review fairness alongside conversion rates.

## Pilot and sustainability

Start with one department, a few participating employers and a bounded challenge set. Keep student access free. Test institutional willingness to pay for workflow coordination and reporting; consider employer-sponsored assessment programs later. Pricing, market size and placement uplift remain unvalidated. Expand only after review capacity, consent and data isolation work reliably.

## Short pitch

“ProofBridge turns industry requirements into a practical path for students. Instead of stopping at a skill list, students submit work, receive a structured human review and see how that evidence fits an internship. Employers can inspect the proof, and colleges can see where support is needed. Our prototype demonstrates the full journey from a missing skill to a reviewed submission and an informed shortlist.”
